# Outcome-first copy

## Audience and lead

The message focuses on parents navigating toddler and preschool situations, with a broader everyday-question scope for the AI helper. This is the recommended audience default from the approved plan, not a newly verified age-coverage claim for the ebooks.

The hero leads with a practical/emotional outcome:

> **Feel less stuck when parenting gets hard.**
>
> Have words to try when feelings run high, meal ideas when dinner is refused, and a clearer starting point when you’re too tired to keep searching.

Only then does a separate paragraph explain the mechanism: two parenting ebooks to keep and an AI parenting helper in Messenger, all for the configured €37/month. The CTA and recurring-payment terms remain directly underneath, so the benefit-led message does not obscure what is being sold.

## Sales sequence and rationale

- **Recognition:** food refusal, leaving the playground, and advice that is harder to apply when tired. “Maybe” language describes possible situations without assigning guilt or treating every parent as the same.
- **Desired change:** words to consider, an idea for dinner and one manageable starting point. No promise that a child stops tantrums, eats differently or obeys.
- **Mechanism:** books as reference material for their two topics; the helper for broader everyday questions and follow-ups. No invented chapters, exercises, reading duration or guide-grounding implementation.
- **Demonstration:** concrete example questions, clearly labelled as examples. No synthetic answers passed off as tested product output. Actual ebook excerpts and tested replies can supply stronger proof only when provided and reviewed.
- **Effort:** choose one current situation and ask one question, rather than completing both books or changing every routine.
- **Offer:** one €37 EUR/month membership, no joining fee, both ebooks kept after cancellation and helper access during the paid period. All amounts still come from the central configuration.
- **Objections:** cover free advice, suitability, AI limitations, Messenger access, the reason for recurring payment and cancellation. No unapproved guarantee, urgency, testimonials or inflated value stack.
- **Closing:** return to a moment the parent wants to feel more prepared for, with a specific next action.

The thank-you page mirrors that intention. It asks the purchaser to choose a situation they want help thinking through, then directs them to the guides, actual Messenger instructions in Whop and copyable starter questions. It remains a static redirect with no payment verification, account backend or automatic access grant.

## Design and measurement

Arial, 18px main body text, dark text on white, existing cover artwork and the single-column layout are retained. A separate mechanism paragraph keeps the subheadline focused on outcomes without hiding the offer.

Three later headline-test alternatives:

1. **Have a starting point for the moments that leave you guessing.**
2. **Feel more prepared for the next difficult parenting moment.**
3. **Words to try. Meal ideas. A place to think through what comes next.**

The first implementation is a hypothesis, not proof of an uplift. Use actual Whop purchases, refunds and renewals to evaluate conversion and retention. CTA clicks and static thank-you views are not verified purchases. Existing analytics remains disabled unless deliberately configured with its consent requirements.

The original-logo asset, real ebook excerpts, external checkout and chatbot implementation remain outside this copy revision. No missing assets or commercial facts are fabricated.
