# Little Steady: audience research and copy decisions

Research retrieved with Firecrawl on 10 November 2026. Intended audience: English-speaking US parents, initially mother-focused creatives, with toddlers and preschoolers. Main guide covers ages 2-6; specialist bonuses cover 18 months-5 years. Public research informs hypotheses; it is not customer research on paying Little Steady members.

## Findings and evidence strength

### Exhaustion and feeling judged: representative population research

Pew surveyed 3,757 US parents of children under 18 in September-October 2022, published January 2023, using probability panels and population weighting. Among respondents with a child under five, 57% found parenting tiring and 35% stressful all or most of the time. These are under-five subgroup findings, not percentages for all mothers aged 25-45 or for a particular ad audience. Mothers across child ages reported more tiredness, stress and judgment than fathers. This supports validating effort rather than implying parental failure. It does not prove guilt, decision fatigue or any specific headline is universal.

Sources: https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/01/24/parenting-in-america-today/ and https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/01/24/gender-and-parenting/

### Food refusal, second meals and worry about nourishment

Mott's April 2024 report uses 1,083 parents of children aged 3-10 from a weighted national probability-panel survey conducted February 2024. The report gives a margin of error of 1-3 percentage points. Picky eating was reported as a healthy-diet challenge by 51%; 61% said they prepare something different when the child dislikes the family meal. Food cost, waste and time also appeared. Ages overlap but extend beyond Little Steady's audience; do not describe these as toddler-only figures. Copy implication: show the work of preparing dinner, the uneaten plate, uncertainty about intake and the decision about another meal. Do not label all selectivity as illness or promise nutritional outcomes.

Source: https://mottpoll.org/reports/balancing-act-providing-healthy-diet-children

### Bedtime difficulty and limited recovery time

Mott's June 2024 report uses 781 parents of children aged 1-6 from the same February survey wave; reported margin of error 2-4 percentage points. Twenty-seven percent described getting their child to bed as difficult; 65% said staying up to play at least occasionally delays sleep. This is parent report, not a clinical sleep diagnosis or a study of this product. The mealtime and bedtime reports are not independent survey waves. Copy implication: bedtime stretching on is a concrete, relevant scene. Needing a break is an empathetic framing, not an outcome measured by the poll. Do not advertise a sleep treatment.

Source: https://mottpoll.org/reports/getting-young-children-bed-sweet-dreams-or-nightmare

### Transitions and screen endings: professional guidance

AAP guidance by Jenny Radesky discusses conflict when enjoyable media stops and recommends planning and calm responses. It supports the relevance of transition and limit-setting examples, not a population prevalence ranking. The guide's remit includes broader ages. Copy implication: switching off a show is another recognizable moment, without asserting addiction or bad parenting.

Source: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx

### Parent language: qualitative illustration

Janet Lansbury's published parent letter describes a three-year-old's morning dressing struggles, repeated attempts and uncertainty about what to do differently. Self-selected comments describe similar situations. This is useful for identifying everyday vocabulary and concrete scenes; it is not representative, and location is not established for every contributor. No identities, comments or testimonials are republished as Little Steady customer evidence. Website copy is original. Reddit search results were not used as full-text evidence because direct retrieval was unsupported.

Source: https://www.janetlansbury.com/2018/05/getting-dressed-daily-struggle/

## Implementation decisions

Keep the relatable calm-versus-exhaustion headline. Replace generic difficulty with repeated shoe requests, food barely touched and bedtime extending. Show the child's difficulty without portraying them as manipulative. Move regret into the emotional recognition section, away from the main sequence of practical scenarios. Mention screen endings and leaving the park as additional examples rather than claiming every family shares them. Balance frustration with affection, practical support and attainable expectations.

No hero jump link or product button. No in-page shortcuts in the header. Readers scroll naturally through recognition, a manageable alternative, product evidence and the complete recurring offer. The global accessibility skip-to-content link remains for keyboard users.

Header: a native modal drawer with privacy information, membership terms, refunds/cancellation and contact/support. Existing configured policy URLs are preferred; otherwise existing information is shown in the drawer. These summaries do not constitute newly researched final legal policies. No invented support address: until supplied, the support section directs existing buyers to their actual purchase confirmation and states that a direct contact will be available before checkout opens.

## Interpretation and next validation

The sources establish relevant themes, not which pain converts best. No defensible single ranking across tantrums, feeding, transitions and sleep is available from these different instruments. Retain general-parenting, tantrum and mealtime ad angles; compare paid conversion and acquisition cost when launch tracking exists. Later interviews should ask about the last difficult moment, what the parent tried, the words they use and what helped. Do not ask leading questions that assume guilt or failure. $29/month remains the owner's launch hypothesis.
